Plains Cree (nêhiyawêwin, ISO 639-3: crk) is an Algic language of the Algonquian branch, spoken mainly across Saskatchewan, Alberta, and Manitoba, with communities in Montana. With roughly 34,000 speakers by Wikidata's estimate (LinguaMeta reports 4,100 — sources disagree) it is the most widely spoken Algonquian language in Canada, yet UNESCO classifies it as severely endangered: most fluent speakers are elders, and immersion schools now lead active revitalization. It is written both in Canadian Aboriginal Syllabics and in Standard Roman Orthography. The grammar is polysynthetic: a single verb can carry the work of an English sentence, every noun is grammatically animate or inanimate, and obviation tracks which third person is in focus across a stretch of discourse. For machine translation, Plains Cree pairs scarce parallel text with unusually strong rule-based tooling — a GiellaLT finite-state analyzer guarantees that generated word forms are valid Cree inflections.
