# Your words, your rules

Language shared in trust has too often been recorded, collected or used to
train someone else's technology — and then held by outsiders, while the
people whose language it is had to ask permission for their own words. The
rails are built so that doesn't happen here.

The rule is simple: **the people who hold the words decide what happens to
them.** The tools write those decisions down, and obey them.

## Where your words go

By default, nowhere. Your files stay on your computer. The tools bring them in
where they are, and show your AI assistant only counts and column names.

[Where your data goes](/your-data) shows every case side by side: your
files, what your assistant is told, a translator's output, a test set you
choose to register, and the few things that do leave your computer — like the
language's code, when the tools look up its public information card.

## The note beside every file

Each file gets a small note, written by the tools from your answers:

- who it belongs to, and whose words are in it;
- whether those people have agreed — *yes*, *not yet*, or *some said no*;
- whether a translator may learn from it;
- whether it may ever leave this computer.

Every tool reads the note and obeys it — including offering a file to someone
else's project, which refuses a private file, or one whose speakers haven't
all agreed. Files start out private. After the
first answer, the tools can only make the rules stricter — except to record a
*yes* from someone who hadn't been asked yet. Loosening a rule is something
the people who hold the words do by hand, on purpose.

## What's built from your words is yours

A translator learns from your sentences, so it carries your rules with it:

- A translator that learned from private sentences is treated as private too,
  because it can repeat what it learned. Its translations stay on your
  computer unless the people who hold the words agree, and say who agreed.
- Champollion claims no ownership of any translator, word list, or anything
  else built from a community's words — and wants none. Writing the tools
  doesn't make us owners of what you build with them.
- Scores can be shared without the sentences. You can publish how well a
  translator did, and keep every sentence private.

## Other people's collections

If you use sentences someone else published, their rules come too:

- **When the tools send sentences out, they follow the licence.** A collection
  whose licence is unusual or unclear is never interpreted on its owners'
  behalf: it isn't sent until its owners say what's allowed. Whether your own
  use is commercial is something no tool can check — that part is yours.
- **Sending sentences to an outside AI is a decision.** Testing an AI model
  means sending it sentences. Collections with a plain non-commercial licence
  go only to services that promise not to learn from what they're sent.
  Collections with unclear terms aren't sent at all until their owners agree.
  Sealed sets never leave the computer that holds them.
- **Champollion doesn't host collections.** The tools point to where a
  collection is published, and fetch it from there. Two exceptions: files
  someone offers through [Join a project](/docs/join-a-project) are stored on
  Champollion's project storage, and a published score for an openly
  licensed test set can include its sentences.

## What no tool can stop

The tools are careful, but some things are up to people:

- **Your AI assistant can open a file if you ask it to.** Most assistants send
  what they read to the company that runs them. For words you want kept
  private, use an assistant that runs on your own computer, or don't ask it to
  open them.
- **Anyone can paste words into a chat.** The tools can't see that happening.
- **The tools can't tell who answered.** When the rails ask who holds a file,
  or whether a speaker agreed, the right person should answer for themselves.

## Our part

We build so that communities can own and control their language data, and
anything made from it. Whether we succeed is for communities to judge — we're
working toward it, and we don't claim we've reached it.

We never name a community, nation or organization as a partner, or as holding
a key, without its consent. If you find your community named here without
that consent, tell us, and we'll remove it.

## Objections and takedowns

If something here is wrong, disrespectful, or uses your community's words or
name in a way you didn't agree to, we want to hear it — in your own words. You
don't need an account.

- **Write to** [info@champollion.dev](mailto:info@champollion.dev), or use
  the site guide's message form (the chat panel → *Send a message*). To ask
  for something to be removed, put **Takedown** in the subject, or choose
  *Takedown* in the form.
- **Our default is to act.** We host almost no one's sentences, so most
  removals are a listing, a citation or a field on a page — and those we take
  down quickly.
- **We say what we did.** If we can't do what you ask, we say why, and who
  decides.
- **Security problems:** [security@champollion.dev](mailto:security@champollion.dev).
