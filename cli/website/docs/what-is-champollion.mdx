# What Champollion Is

**Champollion measures machine translation for languages nobody else measures,
and publishes what it finds.**

That is the whole idea. Everything on this site is either a tool that does that
measuring, the map those measurements build, or the rules we do it under.

---

## The problem

There are roughly 7,000 living languages. For most of them, you cannot answer a
basic question: *if I need this translated, what is the best available way, and
is it good enough to use?*

Not "which company has a demo." Which method — human translator, rule-based
system, prompted language model, trained model — actually works for **this
pair, on this kind of text**, and how do you know.

For English↔French that question has been answered a thousand times. For
English↔Plains Cree, or Ayta Abellen, or thousands of others, it has often
never been asked at all. Coverage gets announced; quality rarely gets shown.

Nobody is measuring, so nobody can tell a real improvement from a press
release — and communities who want to use this technology have no way to check
whether it is safe to trust with their language.

## What we do about it

We are not building a translation engine. Big technology companies build
engines. We build the thing that tells you whether an engine works:

1. **Run a method against a test set** and score it, with the same procedure
   for every method and every language.
2. **Publish the result** with its provenance — what data, what method version,
   what metric, what it cost.
3. **Repeat until the map has shape**: which languages connect to which, how
   strongly, and where the empty space is.

An honest "no method works for this pair yet" is a result we publish. Most of
the map is that answer right now, and saying so is the point — a gap you can
see is a gap someone can fund or fill.

## The rules we work under

These are constraints, not marketing. They shape what the software can do.

**We never hold your corpus.** Test sets are registered by pointing at where
they already live, not by uploading them to us. A method gets scored against a
corpus we never possess. For sealed sets, the data is encrypted on your machine
before anything leaves it, and only a threshold of community key custodians
together can authorize a run.

**Language data is treated like biodata.** The people who provide a corpus hold
the keys to it, and to anything measured against it. Consent is asked for, not
assumed — and it can be withdrawn.

**We are sovereignty-aspirant, not sovereignty-certified.** Whether a project
honours community ownership and control of language data is not a claim that
project gets to make about itself. We do the work and describe the mechanisms
honestly; the determination belongs to the communities involved.

**Every method is welcome, human and machine.** A professional translator, an
FST-based rule system, a prompted frontier model and a fine-tuned NMT model are
compared the same way, on the same test set. We care about getting languages
translated, not about which tool wins.

**We publish what we can't do.** See [Honest
Limitations](/docs/network/honest-limitations) — the list of things this
project does *not* yet claim. Read it before you trust anything else here.

## The pieces, and what each is for

| If you want to… | Use | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Translate an app or site | **[Champollion CLI](/docs/intro)** | One command translates your locale files; you choose the method per language pair. |
| Score a method, or reproduce a score | **[MT-Eval Harness](/docs/network/specifications/harness)** | The Python evaluation engine. Every number on this site comes out of it. |
| Submit a result, or run a contest | **[The Arena](/docs/network/getting-started/submit-a-method)** | The submission door, the database and the contest machinery — where measurement happens. |
| See what is known about a pair | **[The Network](/docs/network/)** | The map the measurements build: coverage, connection strength, routing, the queue of what to measure next. |
| Look up a language | **[The Atlas](/languages)** | <SSOTCount field="catalogLanguageCount" /> language cards — classification, phonology, vitality, and what resources exist. Every value cites its source. |
| Train a model without fooling yourself | **[NMT Forge](/docs/network/getting-started/training-honestly)** | A training suite whose guardrails make the standard self-deception (leaked test data, unrepresentative splits) structurally hard. |
| Work on this as an AI agent | **[Agent Guide](/docs/network/getting-started/agent-guide)** | Tools, the MCP server, and what the machine-readable endpoints contain. |

**Champollion is an index, not an arbiter.** Every value cites its source and
reports what that source actually says. Where sources disagree — and on speaker
counts they very often do — the card shows all of them, attributed. We do not
pick a winner or manufacture a consensus.

## The name

Jean-François Champollion deciphered Egyptian hieroglyphs using the Rosetta
Stone — the same text in three scripts. Parallel text is still how translation
gets learned. The name also carries a tension worth naming out loud: a European
scholar reading someone else's writing, in an era of extraction. We think about
that. It is why the sovereignty rules above are constraints in the software and
not a page in a brochure.
