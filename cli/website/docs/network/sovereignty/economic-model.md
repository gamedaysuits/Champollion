# How the Work Is Funded

> **Executive Summary.** Champollion is a non-commercial research project —
> source-available and free for noncommercial use, its evaluation harness and
> registries open source — and today it is **entirely self-funded by its
> founder**. No grants,
> no sponsors, no institution behind it — yet: we now [actively invite
> sponsors](/get-involved#sponsors). Every sponsorship dollar is
> **100% pass-through**: it funds corpus building, tooling, and community work
> at published rates, publicly accounted — none of it goes to Champollion.
> Nothing here is monetized: there is no paid API, no metering, no revenue
> share, and no platform claim on anything a community owns. This page says
> plainly where the money comes from now, what funding would buy, and how to
> reach us if you want to change the first part.

Champollion is machine-translation research and development tooling —
source-available and free for noncommercial use. The CLI is PolyForm
Noncommercial 1.0.0 (commercial use requires permission), the evaluation
harness is open-source AGPL-3.0, and the data registries are open-source
Apache-2.0. There is no commercial product behind them — and no funding
behind them yet either.

## Where the money comes from today

**One person.** Everything built so far — the harness, the CLI, the language
index, the benchmark specifications, the site — has been self-funded by the
project's founder. We say this plainly for two reasons:

1. **Honesty about scale.** A self-funded project cannot yet pay for the
   corpus construction and speaker validation the specifications cost out. The
   published rates are commitments about *how* money moves when it exists, not
   evidence that it is already moving.
2. **It is an open invitation.** The infrastructure is built and the unit
   costs are published. What's missing is the funding to run it. If you fund
   language technology — as a granting agency, a foundation, a department, or
   an individual — **we want to hear from you**: open an issue on
   [GitHub](https://github.com/gamedaysuits/Champollion) or reach out
   via [champollion.dev](https://champollion.dev).

## What funding buys

The costs are already specified, so a funder can buy concrete, bounded things:

- **A corpus engagement for a language** — $2,500–6,000 in speaker
  compensation ($50–65 CAD/hour, published rates) builds a benchmark corpus
  that stays the builder's property. See
  [How Speakers Get Paid](/docs/network/perspectives/how-speakers-get-paid).
- **A metric-validation round** — $1,475–1,920 pays three bilingual speakers
  to check the automated metrics against human judgment.
- **A sponsored prize** — fund a targeted bar (for example, reliable
  English → Plains Cree). Prize funds are held and awarded by a
  community-governed trust, on the community's terms — not by Champollion.
  See the [Prize Specification](/docs/network/specifications/prizes).
- **Compute and API credits** — pooled to run the public benchmark queue.

In practice this makes the Network a funding-distribution mechanism for
language data work: money in, paid work for the people building corpora out —
and they keep what they build.

## Where money goes

- **To corpus builders and validators, at published rates.** Payment does not
  transfer ownership: a builder is paid for the work *and* remains the steward
  of the corpus.
- **To prize winners, through community trusts.** When a sponsored prize is
  claimed, the trust pays the developer; the method transfers to the community
  under that prize's terms (see [Ownership & Terms](/docs/network/sovereignty/ownership-transfer)).
- **To infrastructure** — hosting, evaluation runs, and maintenance.
- **Publicly accounted.** Sponsored engagements are recorded openly — what was
  funded, at what published rate, and what was delivered — so a sponsor (and
  everyone else) can audit that the pass-through promise held.

## What Champollion takes

**Nothing.** There is no revenue split, no infrastructure percentage, and no
claim on community assets. If a community deploys a method it owns — on its own
servers, through its own channels, commercially or not — everything it earns is
its own. Corpora registered with the Network remain the steward's property
outright, before, during, and after any evaluation.

If commercial opportunities ever arise around this work, we are open to that
conversation — but any such arrangement would be negotiated at that time, with
the stewards whose data or methods are involved, on their terms. Nothing is
pre-committed in these docs, and no document here should be read as reserving a
share of anything for the platform.

## For funders

The sustainability question for language technology is usually "what happens
after the grant ends?" For a non-commercial project the honest answer is:
the *assets* outlive the funding, because they are owned by the people who can
maintain them.

| Traditional model | Stewardship model |
|---|---|
| Grant funds research | Grant funds research |
| Paper published | Corpus built, methods measured |
| Grant ends, tool abandoned | Community owns the corpus and any transferred method outright |
| Community receives nothing | Speakers were paid for every hour; the assets stay home |

Measurable outcomes for a funder:

- Corpora built and registered, under steward control
- Paid speaker-hours delivered to language communities
- Methods measured, and (where a prize's terms say so) transferred to community ownership
- Language pairs covered by trustworthy public benchmarks

See the [Benchmark Specification](/docs/network/specifications/benchmark), §10
for detailed cost models.
