# Contact, Objections & Takedowns

> **Executive summary.** This project is unfinished, on purpose, and forever a
> work in progress. If something here is wrong, extractive, disrespectful of a
> community's rights, or just badly explained — we want to hear it, and we will
> act on it. This page is how you reach us, what we commit to, and how to ask
> that something be corrected or removed. Good objections make this better; they
> are welcome, not tolerated.

## Who this is for

- **Language communities and their representatives** who see their language,
  data, or governance described here and want a correction, a removal, or a
  conversation.
- **Researchers, translators, and dataset stewards** whose work is cited, and
  who want attribution fixed or a listing changed.
- **Anyone** who spots an error, an overclaim, or copy that reads as
  disrespectful.

You do not need an account, and you do not need to be technical. Plain language
in your own words is exactly right.

## What we are (and what that means for corrections)

Champollion is an **index, not an authority.** Every fact on a language card or
in the Network cites a source and reports what that source actually says; where
sources disagree, we show all of them rather than pick a winner. That design has
a direct consequence for you: **if a cited source is wrong, or we've represented
it badly, that is a fixable defect and we treat it as one.** We are not
defending a claim of our own — we are trying to point accurately at someone
else's work, and at the languages themselves.

Our sovereignty posture is **sovereignty-aspirant**: the design is built so that
communities *can* exercise ownership, control, access, and possession of their
data and anything derived from it. Whether it *achieves* that is for communities
to decide — which is exactly why this channel exists.

## How to reach us

| You want to… | Use | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Ask a question, raise an objection, or request a correction | the **site guide's ticket form** (the chat panel → "Send a message"), or email | `info@champollion.dev` |
| Request that data, a citation, or a listing be **removed** | the ticket form (choose **Takedown**), or email with "Takedown" in the subject | `info@champollion.dev` |
| Report a **security vulnerability** | email | `security@champollion.dev` |

The ticket form and the email address reach the same place. The form simply
records your message durably so nothing is lost in transit; you can leave a
reply-to address if you'd like an answer, or stay anonymous if you'd rather not.

## What we commit to

1. **We acknowledge.** If you leave a way to reach you, we aim to confirm we've
   received your message quickly, in plain language, without a legal runaround.
2. **Takedown requests get priority, and the default is to act.** For anything
   we host or index that a rights-holder or community asks us to remove, our
   starting posture is removal, not debate. We host **no corpus content** in the
   first place — corpora are fetched from their source with metadata cards only
   — so most "takedowns" are a citation, a listing, or a card field, and those
   we can correct or pull quickly.
3. **We never name a community, nation, or organization as a partner or
   custodian without its consent.** If you find such a name attached to your
   community without that consent, tell us and we will remove it.
4. **We record objections honestly.** Criticism is as publishable as praise. Our
   honest-limitations page exists precisely so that open problems are visible,
   not buried.
5. **We won't pretend a disagreement is settled.** If we can't do what you ask,
   we'll say so, say why, and say who decides — rather than quietly ignoring it.

## What helps us act faster

You don't need any of this, but it speeds things up:

- A link to the specific page, card, or value.
- What's wrong, in your words, and what right would look like.
- Whether you're speaking for yourself or on behalf of a community or
  organization (and, for a removal, any relationship to the rights-holder — we
  don't require proof, but it helps us prioritize).

## What this channel is *not*

- It is **not** a place to request development help, custom scripts, or
  translations on demand — the project can point you at tooling, but it doesn't
  do engineering work for visitors here.
- It is **not** a way to get a language ranked, promoted, or featured — the
  Network's rankings come only from evaluation runs, never from requests.

We would genuinely rather hear an objection early than have it be right and
unspoken. Thank you for helping keep this honest.
