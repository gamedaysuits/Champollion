# Before you begin

The most important part of building a translator needs no computer at all.
You can do all of it today — even before the tools are out.

## Talk with your speakers

A translator is built from people's words, so start with the people. Tell
them what you want to build and why, who will see their sentences, and that
they can say no. Ask who should be thanked, and how. [Working with
speakers](/docs/working-with-speakers) has more.

## Agree on how to spell

If people write your language in different ways, the translator learns a
muddle. Pick one way for the project — the one a school uses, or your
community's standard — and note which dialect the sentences come from.

## Decide who your words belong to

For each collection of sentences, know who holds it, and what it may be used
for: may a translator learn from it, and may it ever leave your computer?
The rails will ask you these questions. [Your words, your
rules](/docs/your-words-your-rules) explains why they matter.

## Start writing sentences down

Nothing written down yet? A speaker is enough to start. One person says a
sentence in the language; another types it into a spreadsheet, with its
English beside it — two columns, one sentence per row.

Write the kinds of things your family actually says: at home, at school, at
the store, with friends. Short, everyday
sentences teach the most.

## Set some aside for testing

Ask your best speaker to check a few dozen of those sentences carefully, and
keep them in their own file. These are your test sentences — the ones every
translator will be judged on, and never learn from. [Set aside your test
sentences](/docs/your-test-sentences) says why.

## What you'll need later

- A computer — any recent one can start. A newer Mac, or a computer with an
  NVIDIA graphics card, can build the stronger kind of translator.
- Room on its disk. Each stronger translator the tools keep takes more than
  a gigabyte. They check there's room before teaching starts, and can clear
  away earlier rounds' translators that nothing uses.
- An AI assistant that runs as an app on that computer, or someone
  comfortable typing a few commands.

When you have some sentences and the tools are out, you're ready.
