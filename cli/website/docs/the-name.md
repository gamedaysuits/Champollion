**Champollion** is named for Jean-François Champollion, who in 1822 deciphered
Egyptian hieroglyphs and made a writing system legible to the world again. That
is the metaphor we take: restoring access to meaning, and reading a language on
its own terms.

We don't get to take the name without its history.

Champollion worked inside a colonial enterprise. The Rosetta Stone that made his
breakthrough possible reached Europe as military spoils, and remains contested
property today. On his own expedition to Egypt in 1828–29 he had a wall panel cut
out of the tomb of Seti I and shipped to France, where it is still in the Louvre;
his colleague Ippolito Rosellini took a second to Florence. And
"decipherment" itself can be an act of appropriation — an outsider claiming
authority over a people's words. We name this plainly. We are not interested in
laundering it.

What we keep from Champollion is the method, not the man: the conviction that a
language and its writing deserve to be understood on their own terms, and that
the work of access is worth doing well. The extraction we reject — and we built
the tool to be its opposite.

That method is specific. Champollion read the script through Coptic, the last
stage of the Egyptian language, kept alive in the liturgy of the Coptic Church.
The Greek on the Rosetta Stone gave him names; the living tradition gave him the
language. That is the posture we hold to: the people who carry a language are
the way in, and the work goes through them.

## How the tool inverts the extraction

- **We don't take and keep.** Corpora are fetched from their source and never
  hosted by us. We hold metadata, not other people's languages.
- **Ownership stays with communities.** We build toward Indigenous
  data-sovereignty principles — community ownership and control of language
  data — and whether we meet them is for communities to determine, not us.
  For sealed evaluation data, today a sealed set's keys are organizer-held —
  held by whoever organizes it — and Champollion holds no key shares. The
  design lets custodians a community chooses hold the keys under a threshold
  arrangement; community key custodians — none appointed yet.
- **We measure how well translation works; we don't collect languages.** The Network reports how
  well translation works for a given language pair. It is not a trophy case, and
  easy or improperly-scoped data is barred from ranking.
- **We credit the people this rests on** — the linguists, speakers, and corpus
  builders — by name wherever they have agreed to be named, and never name
  anyone who has not.
- **Consent is revocable.** Speakers own their judgments and can withdraw them at
  any time.

We are not an Indigenous organization, and the name says so. Any work we do
for a community is in a service capacity, and only with its consent (no community
has engaged us yet) — and a non-Indigenous project
carrying this name has to earn trust rather than assert it. We expect scrutiny
and welcome the critique. A community can tell us the name does harm, and that
objection will be heard: write to
[info@champollion.dev](mailto:info@champollion.dev).
