# Host a sealed test

Sometimes the best translator for your language will come from someone else —
a researcher, a student, a company. A **sealed test** lets them prove it on
*your* sentences, without ever seeing those sentences.

## How it works

Your test sentences are locked with a key, on your computer. People who want
to compete send in their translators. Your computer runs each one on the
locked sentences, and only the scores come out — never the sentences, and
never the translations.

So the test stays a real test: nobody can study it or tune to it, because
nobody can read it.

## What you decide

You set the terms, before anyone competes:

- **What counts as good enough** to win.
- **What happens to a winning method:**
  - **it becomes yours** — it passes to you, the people who hold the test;
  - **its maker keeps it** — you score it, and keep at most a locked copy for
    checking;
  - **its maker keeps it, but shares it with everyone** — they must publish it
    openly.
- **Whether there's a prize** at all, and who funds it. Champollion takes no
  share of any prize.

## What exists today

The tools for sealed tests work: locking a set, running submitted
translators on a computer cut off from the internet, and publishing only
scores. But this is **not on the rails yet** — today it means following a
longer manual, not answering an assistant's questions step by step.

Who holds the key today: whoever locks the set. No community has yet named
people to hold the key together, and Champollion holds no part of any key.

Open sealed tests, when there are any, are listed on the
[shared tasks](/shared-tasks) page.
